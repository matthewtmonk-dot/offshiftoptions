## 5. Biggest caveats
1. **Survivorship**: only currently listed tickers. Stocks that fell and were delisted/bankrupt are absent, so tail rates after oversold signals are understated, and guards aimed at falling-knife situations (G1-G3 especially) are *systematically disadvantaged* in measurement - the very stocks most likely to be flagged and to crash further are partly missing. Absolute rates and guard value are biased in an unknown direction; this cannot be fixed with this data.
2. **Universe is hand-built**, not the scanner universe (not available offline); selection of "names I remember" is itself a form of hindsight (tickers that are well-known today). Includes some ADRs/foreign filers; ETF/leveraged/preferred products deliberately excluded.
3. **Overlapping windows / serial dependence**: a stock oversold for several consecutive Fridays contributes overlapping 10-session windows (5 shared sessions). Week-block bootstrap handles cross-sectional (same-week) dependence but NOT same-stock dependence across adjacent weeks, so p-values are somewhat optimistic. A first-of-run sensitivity for p0 is given above.
4. **Regime clustering**: tail events cluster in market-wide selloffs (Mar 2020, 2022, Apr 2025). With few independent episodes, year-level "consistency" is weak evidence. 2026 validation year is partial.
5. **Yahoo quirks**: OHLC are split-adjusted but not dividend-adjusted (small bias in indicators for high yielders, ignored); split reconstruction depends on Yahoo split events being complete and correct (unadjusted spin-offs, mergers, ticker reuse or reverse-split errors can create bogus price levels - see residual jump list in section 1); volume revisions are possible; intraday lows can include bad ticks. As-traded price band uses the reconstructed price.
6. **Period asymmetry**: bars start Nov 2016 and 252 sessions of history are required, so the effective development period is ~Nov 2017-2022 (~5 years, including the 2020 crash and 2022 bear market) and validation is ~3.8 years of mostly rising market.
7. **Entry realism**: entry at Monday open assumes a fill at the open price; weekend gaps are part of the outcome; the primary outcome uses intraday lows (not closes). No transaction costs, no options pricing - this is a stock-price tail study only, not an options P&L study.
8. **Multiple comparisons / one-sided test**: p-value tests only p0 - pg > 0 (not >= 2pp). Four guards Bonferroni-adjusted to 1.25%.
9. **Interpretive choices where the protocol was silent** (see final report): per-year support rule; MIN 20 flagged obs per year; %B/RSI computed from series start (Wilder seeding); day-1 = entry day for 5/10-session horizons; weeks defined Monday-Sunday; window completeness required.

## 6. Interpretation (added after the run; no parameters changed)

- Fetch: 523 symbols requested, 487 downloaded, 36 HTTP 404 (delisted/renamed); 468 had usable history (>=300 bars); 365 distinct stocks produced at least one baseline signal. Result is complete (not partial) for this universe, with no rate-limit blocking.
- Oversold screen vs control: p0 = 22.4% vs 21.1% (Ctrl-all) overall; development 25.3% vs 22.0% (one-sided block-bootstrap p = 0.035, not significant at a strict level and driven by 2020: 41.4% vs 26.4%); validation 18.7% vs 19.9% (screen is NOT riskier than control). Vs the volatility-matched non-signal control the screen is slightly higher (22.4% overall vs 18.8%), but that control is matched by construction on a volatility range and the comparison uses only same-slice IQR. Conclusion: no robust evidence that the RSI/%B screen itself carries a higher 10% tail rate than comparable eligible stocks; most of the ~20% base rate is just the volatility of this $10-50 liquid universe.
- Guards (validation, binding): none shows a reduction. G1 0.0 pp, G2 -0.1 pp, G3 -0.8 pp (flagged names had a LOWER tail rate), G4 -0.1 pp. All four mechanically DROP (all had conclusive samples). G2 additionally fails retention (45% < 60%).
- G2 (falling trend) worked in development (p0 25.3% -> pg 19.8%, 5.4 pp, boot p < 0.0001, 3 supporting years) but the effect vanished in validation (ratio 0.99x). This is the type of in-sample finding that does not replicate; note dev G2 largely reflects the 2022 bear market and 2019/2021 and the dev result is also not used for decisions.
- Survivor-only caveat applies to all of the above: absence of a validation effect could be partly a survivorship artifact, and presence of one in development could be too; neither period can support a hard rejection.
